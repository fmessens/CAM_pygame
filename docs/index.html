From light to game geometry

The software compares what it sent to the projector with what the camera actually sees.

  1. Align the images

    Project a checkerboard and find its corners with OpenCV. A red dot marks the upper-left square so the board can be oriented. The fitted homography H maps projector coordinates to camera coordinates.

  2. Predict the brightness

    Capture black and white projections to estimate a gain and offset for each camera pixel. Warp the game frame with H and apply those values to predict the camera image inside the projection area.

  3. Recover the shadows

    In Shadow Harbor and Water Wakes, pixels sufficiently darker than predicted form a shadow mask. OpenCV extracts the contours and H−1 maps them back to the playfield as polygons.

Two ways to simulate water

Wave dynamics · Level 1

Shadow Harbor

A 240 × 180 NumPy grid stores wave height. Each step uses the current and previous heights plus a five-point Laplacian, which compares a cell with its four neighbors.

unext = (2 − d)u − (1 − d)uprev + c²Δ5u

Shadows, the dock, and the grid edges pin the height to zero. Clicks and a driven source generate ripples. The game samples the surface slope to help steer the boat.

Fluid dynamics · Level 6 bis

Water Wakes

A 128 × 72 grid stores horizontal and vertical velocity. Semi-Lagrangian advection traces each cell backward through the flow and bilinearly samples the old velocity.

∇²p ≈ ∇·v  ·  v ← v − ∇p

A 35-iteration Jacobi pressure solve reduces divergence, and solid shadow cells have zero velocity. A procedural wake adds slowing and sideways motion downstream. Bubbles trace the resulting flow.

Set up the arcade

Connect a projector as an extended display and point a USB camera at the projected playfield. The default projection target is display 2.

python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python level_menu.py --camera-index 1

Choose Shadow Calibration in the menu. Keep the red dot in view and press Space when the checkerboard is in focus. The levels share the saved calibration. Run it again if the camera or projector moves.
